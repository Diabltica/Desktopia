# Desktopia – motherboard

KiCad 10 project: `desktopia.kicad_sch`, `desktopia.kicad_pcb`. Firmware: [../../firmware/desktopia/](../../firmware/desktopia/README.md).

The motherboard sits under the desk top. It takes the 24 V supply and passes it through a current shunt and a MOSFET
switch to its own H-bridge (DRV8701E gate driver, four N-MOSFETs), which drives the motor. The bridge can be bypassed:
J5 / J6 still give the switched 24 V and DIR / PWM to an external driver. The board measures the height, powers the HMI
board and talks to it.

Reading the schematic: connections are drawn as wires. A label only appears where a net leaves one block for another,
at the MCU pin and at the block it reaches (`TOF_INT`, `SCL`, `SDA`, `Mpwm`, `Mdir`, `nSLEEP`, `nFAULT`, `MOTOR_PWR`,
`I_ALERT`, `RESET`, `+5V_HMI`, `MOTOR_SW`). `MOTOR_24V` only names a 15 A net. In the H-bridge block, `OUT1` / `OUT2`
join the two legs to J7, `LS_SRC` joins the low-side sources to the shunts R27 / R28 under U7, and `VM` names the
bridge supply after JP6. Wires that cross without a dot are not connected: MISO over SCK / MOSI at J2, D+ over D- at
the USB connector, Vin+ over Vin- at the INA226, DI over DE at U5.

Colours: each block has its own background and title colour (24 V input and 5 V supply, MCU, height sensor, HMI link,
motor supply, H-bridge). Wires and labels take the colour of their net class, with a legend on the sheet, left of the
MCU:

## Connectors

| Ref | Function | Pin-out |
|---|---|---|
| J1 | 24 V supply input | 1 = +24 V, 2 = GND |
| J2 | ISP | 1 = MISO, 2 = +5 V, 3 = SCK, 4 = MOSI, 5 = RESET, 6 = GND |
| J3 | USB-C: flashing and debug logs | USB 2.0 full speed |
| J4 | RJ11 cord to the HMI board | 1 = +5 V, 2 = GND, 3 = A, 4 = B, 5 = GND, 6 = +5 V |
| J5 | supply out to an external motor driver (after the shunt and Q5) | 1 = MOTOR_SW, 2 = GND |
| J6 | external motor driver command | 1 = DIR (`Mdir`, PC6), 2 = PWM (`Mpwm`, PB6 / OC1B), 3 = GND (the driver's logic ground) |
| J7 | motor, from the on-board H-bridge | 1 = OUT1 (Q6 / Q7), 2 = OUT2 (Q8 / Q9) |

The J4 pin-out is symmetric, so a reversed telephone cord only swaps A and B, never +5 V and GND. The HMI firmware
detects and corrects the swap. Use 6-conductor (6P6C) cords only, and never plug J4 into a telephone wall socket.

The four terminals unplug. The board has the header; the wires are screwed into a plug, so the board can be fitted or
removed without touching a screw under the desk. JLCPCB solders the headers; buy the plugs with the parts (LCSC), they
are not in the assembly BOM. Each header carries its plug in the `Plug` field of the schematic.

| Ref | Header (on the board) | Plug (on the cable) | Rating | Wire |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| J1, J5 | Cixi Kefa KF2EDGRC-5.08-2P (C441236), 5.08 mm, right angle, closed ends | KF2EDGK-5.08-2P-AA (C440880) | 15 A, 300 V | up to 2.5 mm² (12 AWG) |
| J7 | Cixi Kefa KF2EDGRC-7.62-2P (C441246), 7.62 mm, right angle, closed ends | KF2EDGK-7.62-2P (C440890) | 15 A, 400 V | up to 2.5 mm² (12 AWG) |
| J6 | KANGNEX WJ15EDGRC-3.81-03P-14-00A (C8406), 3.81 mm, right angle, closed ends | WJ15EDGK-3.81-03P-14-00A (C8413) | 8 A, 300 V | thin command wires |

- Use 1.5 mm² (16 AWG) or more on J1 / J5 / J7, with ferrules on stranded wire.
- The plugs only go in one way round, and the closed ends stop a plug from sliding sideways over the wrong pins.
- J7 has another pitch on purpose: the 24 V plug (5.08 mm) cannot go into the motor output, where it would feed the
  bridge from its outputs, and the motor plug (7.62 mm) cannot go into J1 or J5. J1 and J5 share a part (both carry
  the 24 V, and J5 is only used with the bridge bypassed). J6 is smaller still: its 3-pin 3.81 mm plug does not fit the
  5.08 mm headers, and the 5.08 mm plugs are too thick for J6. Check that on the first parts anyway, and mark the
  plugs.
- 15 A is the rating of these blocks (the old fixed KF128 terminals took 24 A). It matches the rest of the motor path:
  the 15 A fuse in the supply cable and the 14.6 A current limit of the bridge.

The MD10C joins its logic and power grounds,
so the ground wire of J6 runs in parallel with the one of J5. It carries a share of the motor current, set by the ratio
of the wire resistances: about 10 %, so 1 A at 10 A, with 24 AWG on J6 and 14 AWG on J5 of the same length. The wire
handles that easily. On the PCB, this current flows from J6 pin 3 to J5 pin 2 (see *PCB: what to do before ordering*).

## Power

- Input: 24 V, 28 V at most (D2 starts to conduct at 31.1 V; the on-board bridge accepts 45 V, an external MD10C only
  30 V). Size the supply for the motor (the on-board bridge is sized for 15 A, the MD10C for 13 A continuous) plus a few
  watts for the electronics.
- Motor path, sized for 15 A: J1 / J5 / J7 pluggable terminals 15 A, R23 3 W (0.45 W at 15 A, 1.8 W at 30 A), R27 / R28
  3 W each (0.45 W each at 15 A, only while the bridge drives), Q5 90 A (1.1 W at 15 A, 0.12 W at 5 A), C6 100 µF
  50 V bulk, D2 600 W TVS. Give J1 → R23 → Q5 → JP6 → H-bridge → J7 (and → J5), R27 / R28 and the ground return
  copper for 15 A or more.
- C6 damps the supply cable: plugged in live, the cable inductance and the ceramic C5 ring up to about twice the
  input, 40 V or more, which is the absolute maximum of U2 and U6. What damps it is the ESR of an aluminium
  electrolytic (0.3 to 1 Ω), more than its capacitance. A simulation with a 0.7 to 3 µH cable gives a peak of 24 to
  28 V with C6 (ESR about 0.6 Ω), and 38 to 43 V with ceramics only. So C6 cannot be replaced by ceramics, nor by a
  polymer or hybrid capacitor, whose ESR is too low.
- Motor supply switch (Q5, high side, no relay): the firmware switches the driver on for a move and off after 5 minutes
  without one, or at once on an INA226 alert. R21 / R20 split the 24 V, so Q5 sees V<sub>GS</sub> = −12 V; D3 clamps
  it at 15 V, under the ±20 V maximum, even with a 28 V input. C21 (gate-drain) makes Q5 turn on in about 15 ms, so the
  driver's input capacitors charge without an inrush spike; the firmware waits 100 ms before the first PWM. Turning off
  takes about 20 ms (R21 alone discharges the gate). The firmware only does it with the PWM at 0, so Q5 never opens
  under load. On an overcurrent, the PWM cut is what stops the current, and Q5 then isolates the driver. **Q5 is not a
  fuse**: if the driver itself fails short, Q5 opens too slowly to protect it. Keep a fuse (15 A) in the 24 V supply
  cable. D4 clamps J5 when the driver's cable inductance pulls MOTOR_SW below ground at turn-off. The P-MOSFET's body
  diode lets the motor's braking energy flow back to C6 when Q5 is off.
- 5 V rail (TPS5430): MCU, transceiver, INA226, and the HMI board through F1 (500 mA PTC). L1 (1.8 A saturation) and
  D1 (3 A) allow 1.5 A, about twice the worst case (HMI at the PTC limit + this board).
- The 5 V output uses ceramic capacitors, so the TPS5430 needs the external compensation of TI's SLVA237 (C4, R9, C12,
  C11). A model of the loop (TPS5430 type III network + LC filter + this network) gives a 14 to 27 kHz crossover and
  more than 60° of phase margin from 0.1 to 1.5 A, with ±25 % on the output capacitance.
- 2.8 V rail (XC6206P282, ±2 %): VL53L1CB, its pull-ups and the gates of Q1 / Q2. 2.8 V is the sensor's typical
  supply (recommended 2.6 to 3.5 V, 3.6 V absolute maximum, datasheet DS12385). That leaves 0.7 V of headroom under the
  maximum; 3.3 V would only leave 0.13 V in the worst case.

## H-bridge

The on-board motor driver replaces the external Cytron MD10C R3, which is limited to 20 kHz PWM and 13 A. It is built
for 15 A and a PWM above hearing (25 kHz), at the lowest part cost.

- **Driver**: U7 DRV8701E (TI SLVSCX5B), PH/EN interface: PH = `Mdir`, EN = `Mpwm`, the same sign-magnitude command
  as the MD10C. EN low = brake (both low sides on, slow decay). PWM up to 100 kHz; 25 kHz only costs about 25 % more
  switching losses than 20 kHz. VM 5.9 to 45 V (47 V absolute maximum). Logic inputs: V<sub>IH</sub> 1.5 V, 5.5 V
  maximum, so the 5 V MCU drives them directly. Its charge pump (VM + 9.5 V) allows 100 % duty.
- **FETs**: Q6-Q9 Infineon BSC028N06NS (C148250): 60 V, 2.8 mΩ maximum at 10 V, Q<sub>g</sub> 37 nC, Q<sub>gd</sub>
  7 nC typical. At 15 A, about 0.95 W of conduction in each conducting FET (hot R<sub>DS(on)</sub> ≈ 1.5 × 2.8 mΩ),
  plus about 0.5 W of switching in the high side that switches, at 25 kHz. The charge pump supplies
  37 nC × 2 × 25 kHz ≈ 2 mA, far under its rating (TI: 200 nC FETs at 40 kHz). Avoid the look-alikes: HXY sells a
  "BSC028N06NS" with 74 nC of gate charge. The TI CSD18540Q5B fits too but cost $3.26 at JLCPCB in November 2026.
- **Gate drive**: IDRIVE left open = 100 mA source / 200 mA sink, about 70 ns rise and 35 ns fall with 7 nC of
  Q<sub>gd</sub>. No gate resistors.
- **Current limit (DRV8701, cycle by cycle)**: R27 + R28 (2 × 2 mΩ 3 W in series = 4 mΩ, R23's part) sit between
  the low-side sources (`LS_SRC`) and GND. SP / SN read the voltage across both from their Kelvin pads (pad 2 of R27,
  pad 3 of R28). VREF = 4.92 V × 3.3k / 13.3k = 1.22 V (R29 / R30 from +5V), so
  I<sub>CHOP</sub> = (V<sub>REF</sub> − V<sub>OFF</sub>) / (A<sub>V</sub> × R<sub>SENSE</sub>)
  = (1.22 − 0.05) / (20 × 4 mΩ) ≈ 14.6 A typical. Above it the bridge brakes (both low sides on) for
  t<sub>OFF</sub> = 25 µs, then drives again: start-up and stall peaks stay near 15 A whatever the motor does.
  - Tolerance: V<sub>OFF</sub> is 50 mV typical but up to 250 mV, A<sub>V</sub> 18 to 22 (16 to 24 under 50 mV of
    sense voltage), +5V ±2 %, resistors ±1 %: 9.5 to 18 A in the worst case. One 2 mΩ shunt alone would give 7 to
    20 A (30 mV at 15 A, where the gain and the offset weigh twice as much); a second R23 keeps the part number.
  - VREF comes from +5V (4.92 V ±2 %), not from AVDD as in TI's example: AVDD is only 4.4 to 5.2 V. The VREF
    absolute maximum is 5.75 V, so the 1.22 V stays harmless while VM is off or the bridge is bypassed (0.37 mA in
    the divider).
  - R30 sets the level: 4.7k → 19 A (VREF 1.57 V). Keep VREF ≥ 0.3 V (accuracy).
  - TP14 is SO, the bridge current while driving: SO = V<sub>OFF</sub> + 80 mV/A. With the bridge awake and stopped,
    SO = V<sub>OFF</sub>: measure it on the first board, the real limit is (V<sub>REF</sub> − V<sub>OFF</sub>) / 80 mV/A
    (± 10 % of gain left).
  - The shunts only carry the current while the bridge drives: in brake (EN low, or chopping) the current loops
    through the two low sides. 0.9 W in all at 15 A and 100 % duty.
- **INA226 and V<sub>DS</sub> monitor**: the INA226 still cuts the PWM and Q5 above `MOTOR_CURRENT_LIMIT_MA` (8 A),
  from the supply current averaged over 8.8 ms. The DRV8701 caps the bridge current within microseconds in between,
  and on a start-up. With the motor blocked, chopping holds about 14.6 A in the motor while the supply sees less
  (current × drive time), so the INA226 may not trip: the firmware's stall fault (duty up, height not moving) stops
  it. The DRV8701 also keeps its V<sub>DS</sub> monitor (about 1 V across a driven FET for 4.5 µs → all FETs off,
  retry after 3 ms, `nFAULT` low): with 2.8 mΩ FETs, a short-circuit protection.
- **Sleep and faults**: `nSLEEP` (PB5, D9) has an internal pull-down (115 kΩ typical), so the bridge stays off, FETs
  held off, while the MCU is in reset, in the bootloader or blank. TI asks for nSLEEP to go high only once VM is up:
  set it after `MOTOR_SW` (the firmware already waits 100 ms after Q5), wait 1 ms (t<sub>WAKE</sub>), then start the
  PWM; set it low before Q5 opens. `nFAULT` (PB7, D11, PCINT7) is open drain with no resistor on the board: enable the
  MCU pull-up (20 to 50 kΩ, TI asks ≥ 10 kΩ). It goes low on VM or charge pump undervoltage, overcurrent, pre-driver
  fault and overtemperature.
- **Bulk**: at 15 A and 50 % duty the input capacitors carry I × √(D (1 − D)) ≈ 7.5 A RMS, far more than C6 (general
  purpose) can. C24 / C25 (2 × 470 µF 50 V, Rubycon ZLJ low impedance series, 10 × 20 mm radial, C2902875) and
  C26 / C27 (10 µF 50 V ceramic) take it. Check their ripple rating in the ZLJ datasheet if the climbs get long;
  desk moves are short. C24 / C25 sit behind Q5, which is off at plug-in (its body diode blocks), so they play no part
  in the hot-plug ringing that C6 damps (see *Power*). Q5's 15 ms soft start charges them with about 1.5 A.
- **Bypass**: to drive the motor with an external driver on J5 / J6 instead, cut JP2-JP5 (bridged solder jumpers on PH,
  EN, nSLEEP, nFAULT) and the JP6 wire link (VM). The bridge is then disconnected, logic and power: a dead DRV8701 or a
  shorted FET cannot load DIR / PWM or `MOTOR_SW`. To bring it back: solder blobs on JP2-JP5, a wire on JP6. JP6 is two
  1.7 mm holes 6 mm apart for a 1.5 mm² wire (15 A), soldered by hand like U4: it is not in the BOM, so the bridge has
  no VM until it is fitted.

## I2C bus

| Device | Address | Side |
|---|---|---|
| INA226 (U6) | 0x40 (A0 = A1 = GND) | 5 V |
| VL53L1CB (U3) | 0x29 (0x52 in 8-bit form) | 2.8 V, behind Q1 / Q2 |

SDA = PD1, SCL = PD0. Pull-ups: 3.3k R1 (SCL) / R5 (SDA) to 5 V, and R2 / R6 to 2.8 V on the sensor side of Q1 / Q2.
Q1 / Q2 are the classic MOSFET level shifter (NXP AN10441): gate on +2V8, source on the sensor side, drain on the 5 V
side. The BSS138 (Vgs(th) 1.5 V max) turns fully on with 2.8 V on its gate, so a low from the MCU reaches the sensor at
about 0.2 V, under its 0.6 V VIL. A 2N7002 (Vgs(th) up to 2.5 V) would only pass it through its body diode, around 0.8 V.
At 400 kHz the 3.3k pull-ups give about 220 ns of rise time, under the 300 ns of the fast-mode specification (ST
suggests 3.6k up to 90 pF).

Q3 is the same circuit on the VL53L1CB GPIO1 (data-ready interrupt, R11 10k pull-up to 2.8 V). It gives `TOF_INT` on
PB4 (D8, pin-change interrupt PCINT4), with R10 10k pull-up to 5 V. The MCU sees a full 5 V high level instead of
2.8 V, which is under the ~3 V input threshold of the ATmega. The firmware polls the sensor over I2C and does not use
`TOF_INT` yet.

The ATmega32U4 has five external interrupts, all taken. INT0 / INT1 (PD0 / PD1) are the I2C and INT2 / INT3
(PD2 / PD3) the RS-485 UART; both are fixed by the hardware. INT6 (PE6) is kept for the INA226 alert (`I_ALERT`).
`TOF_INT` therefore uses a pin-change interrupt. It is also a hardware interrupt, with the same latency, but
`attachInterrupt()` does not cover it: configure PCICR / PCMSK0, or simply read PB4. PB5 is `nSLEEP` and PB7 `nFAULT`
(its PCINT7 can flag a bridge fault). PB0 is free but is the RX LED pin of the Arduino core (`board = micro`), which drives
it on USB traffic.
The INA226 settings are R<sub>shunt</sub> = 2 mΩ, current LSB = 1.25 mA, calibration = 2048. It reads 0.34 W in the
shunt at 13 A.

## Assembly (JLCPCB)

Every assembled part has its LCSC number in the `LCSC` and `jlcpcb PartNumber` fields. JLCPCB charges a loading fee
(about $3 per order) for each Extended part number. Basic parts have none, and Preferred parts have none in Economic
PCBA.

- Extended: C6 (100 µF bulk), L1, F1, J3 (USB-C), J1 / J5 (one part number), J7, J4, J6, Q5 (motor supply switch),
  R23 / R27 / R28 (2 mΩ 3 W shunt, C154685, one part number), U1 (2.8 V LDO), U3 (VL53L1CB), U6 (INA226), U7 (DRV8701ERGER, C90964), Q6-Q9 (BSC028N06NS, C148250, one part
  number), C24 / C25 (Rubycon 50ZLJ470M10X20, C2902875, radial: through-hole like the terminals). JLCPCB has no Basic
  power inductor, electrolytic capacitor, fuse, connector, shunt, 2.8 V regulator, gate driver or power MOSFET. Q5
  (IPD90P04P4-05, C536914) cost $1.94 with 469 in stock in October 2026: check the stock before ordering. Any −40 V
  P-MOSFET in TO-252 with R<sub>DS(on)</sub> ≤ 10 mΩ at −10 V fits the footprint. Any 60 V N-MOSFET in SuperSO8 / 5 × 6
  with R<sub>DS(on)</sub> ≤ 3 mΩ at 10 V and Q<sub>g</sub> ≤ 60 nC fits Q6-Q9 (onsemi NTMFS5C628NL, C145537, for
  example): check the gate charge in the maker's own datasheet, look-alikes of the same name may have twice as much.
- Preferred: D2 (TVS), D3 (Zener), Q1 / Q2 / Q3 / Q4 (BSS138). Everything else is Basic: the H-bridge passives reuse

## PCB rules

Board: 2 layers, FR-4 1.6 mm, 1 oz (35 µm) outer copper, HASL, green solder mask, tented vias, made by JLCPCB. The
values come from JLCPCB's [capabilities](https://jlcpcb.com/capabilities/pcb-capabilities) (read in November 2026) and
IPC-2221B. They live in three places:

- *Board Setup → Constraints*: JLCPCB's absolute minimums, the floor under every rule. 0.10 mm track and gap; via
  0.45 mm with a 0.2 mm hole and a 0.05 mm ring; 0.2 mm hole to hole and hole to copper; 0.3 mm copper to board edge
  (JLCPCB: 0.2 mm for a routed edge); 0.1 mm solder mask web; silkscreen text 1 mm high, 0.15 mm stroke.
- *Board Setup → Net Classes*: for each class, the routing width, clearance, via and differential pair (table below).
  The same widths and vias are in the track and via lists of the toolbar.
- [`desktopia.kicad_dru`](desktopia.kicad_dru) (*Board Setup → Custom Rules*): the DRC minimum width and via size of
  each class, JLCPCB's hole rules, and two exceptions. It replaces the generic JLCPCB template, whose clearance rules
  (0.127 mm between tracks, between pads) overrode the net class clearances: in KiCad a matching custom rule wins.

| Class | Track: routing / DRC min | Clearance | Via: pad / hole | A track carries (ΔT 10 / 20 °C) | A via carries (ΔT 10 / 20 °C) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Default` | 0.25 / 0.15 mm | 0.20 mm | 0.6 / 0.3 mm | 0.9 / 1.2 A | 0.7 / 1.0 A |
| `Buses` | 0.25 / 0.15 mm, pairs 0.25 mm with a 0.2 mm gap | 0.20 mm | 0.6 / 0.3 mm | 0.9 / 1.2 A | 0.7 / 1.0 A |
| `Motor_command` | 0.25 / 0.15 mm | 0.20 mm | 0.6 / 0.3 mm | 0.9 / 1.2 A | 0.7 / 1.0 A |
| `Current_sense` | 0.25 / 0.15 mm, pair gap 0.25 mm | 0.25 mm | 0.6 / 0.3 mm | 0.9 / 1.2 A | 0.7 / 1.0 A |
| `Power_2V8` | 0.3 / 0.2 mm | 0.20 mm | 0.6 / 0.3 mm | 1.0 / 1.4 A | 0.7 / 1.0 A |
| `HV_signal` | 0.3 / 0.2 mm | 0.25 mm | 0.6 / 0.3 mm | 1.0 / 1.4 A | 0.7 / 1.0 A |
| `Gate_drive` | 0.4 / 0.25 mm | 0.25 mm | 0.6 / 0.3 mm | 1.2 / 1.7 A | 0.7 / 1.0 A |
| `Power_5V` | 0.5 / 0.3 mm | 0.20 mm | 0.8 / 0.4 mm | 1.4 / 2.0 A | 0.9 / 1.2 A |
| `GND` | 0.5 / 0.25 mm | 0.20 mm | 0.8 / 0.4 mm (0.6 / 0.3 allowed for stitching) | 1.4 / 2.0 A | 0.9 / 1.2 A |
| `Switch_node` | 1.0 / 0.6 mm | 0.25 mm | 0.8 / 0.4 mm | 2.4 / 3.2 A | 0.9 / 1.2 A |
| `Power_24V` | 3.0 / 0.25 mm | 0.25 mm | 1.0 / 0.5 mm | 5.3 / 7.2 A | 1.0 / 1.4 A |

- **Current**: IPC-2221, I = k × ΔT^0.44 × A^0.725 (A in mil²), k = 0.048 for an outer track. A via barrel counts as an
  inner conductor (k = 0.024) with JLCPCB's 18 µm of hole plating. Size continuous loads for ΔT 10 °C. The 5 V rail
  (1.5 A at most) takes 0.5 mm and two vias per layer change; a gate track carries 0.2 A peaks, its 0.4 mm is for a low
  inductance.
- **15 A path** (`Power_24V` and its GND return): one 3 mm track only carries 5 to 7 A. For 15 A at ΔT 20 °C, IPC-2221
  asks for 8.3 mm on one layer, or 3.2 mm on each layer with both layers carrying the current (6.5 mm or 2.5 mm at
  ΔT 30 °C). So draw it as pours on both layers, stitched; the 3 mm width is for short links. A layer change takes
  11 vias of 1.0 / 0.5 mm (1.4 A each at ΔT 20 °C). The DRC minimum of the class is 0.25 mm because its nets also
  reach small pins (U7 VM / SH1 / SH2, U6 pin 8, D4, TP13): DRC cannot tell the 15 A path from a branch, so check it by
  eye.
- **Clearance** (IPC-2221B table 6-1): 0.2 mm for the classes under 15 V (A6, uncoated pads: 0.13 mm). 0.25 mm for
  the 24 V classes (A6, 16-30 V): `Power_24V`, `Switch_node`, `HV_signal`, `Gate_drive`, and `Current_sense`, whose
  Vin+ / Vin- sit at 24 V. Tracks under the solder mask only need 0.05 mm up to 30 V (B4). The gate and charge pump
  nets reach VM + 10.5 V, about 38 V. Their only bare copper is the U7 pins and the FET gate pads, and the copper next
  to those stays within 10.5 V of them.
- **Vias**: 0.6 / 0.3 mm at least everywhere. JLCPCB charges extra for holes under 0.2 mm, and for 0.2-0.25 mm holes in
  pads under 0.45 mm. The power classes get larger vias.
- **Exceptions** (custom rules):
  - Pads of one footprint keep the maker's land pattern, at 0.15 mm at least. This covers the INA226 pins, the
    DRV8701 pins next to its exposed pad, and the USB-C GND pads next to the connector's own locating holes.
  - Tracks inside the courtyard of U6, U7 or J3 may pass 0.15 mm from other copper, to leave their 0.5 mm pitch pins.
- **Other JLCPCB rules** in the file:
  - holes 0.2 to 6.3 mm, NPTH 0.5 mm at least;
  - PTH annular ring 0.18 mm (0.25 mm recommended); the 0.2 mm thermal vias of U2 / U7 count as vias;
  - pad holes of two footprints 0.45 mm apart (holes of one footprint keep the 0.2 mm via spacing), PTH hole to
    track 0.35 mm;
  - silkscreen 0.15 mm from pads.
- DRC on the current board (all parts placed, nothing routed) only shows what was there before the rules: 2 courtyard
  overlaps, an invalid board outline and silkscreen warnings. The rules were checked on test tracks: a 24 V track
  0.22 mm from a signal, a 0.2 mm 5 V track and a 0.6 / 0.3 mm via on a 24 V net are flagged; a track leaving an
  INA226 pin, a 0.15 mm I2C track and a 0.6 / 0.3 mm GND via pass.

## PCB: what to do before ordering

The PCB has every part (*Update PCB from Schematic* of November 11, 2026, with the pluggable headers), but nothing is
routed. Run *Update PCB from Schematic* once more: it renames the H-bridge supply `Net-(JP6-B)` → `VM`, which puts it
in `Power_24V`, and adds the mounting hole H4, which the PCB does not have yet (DRC schematic parity, November 11,
2026). Place J1 / J5 / J6 / J7 again on the board edge. For the
layout:

- Place C5 right against VIN / GND of U2 (TI asks for a ceramic within 1 mm). Put D2 and C6 next to J1.
- Keep the buck loop tight: C7 / C8 next to L1 and the ground of D1. Put C4, C11, R9 and C12 next to the VSENSE pin
  of U2, away from the PH node and L1.
- J1 / J5 / J7 / J6 use the `Desktopia:PluggableHeader_*` footprints: KiCad's Phoenix Contact ones, since the Kefa and
  KANGNEX headers copy them (MSTBA 2,5/2-G-5,08 for J1 / J5, GMSTBA 2,5/2-G-7,62 for J7, MC 1,5/3-G-3,81 for J6;
  1.4 / 1.4 / 1.2 mm holes), with the mated plug added to the 3D view and its outline on F.Fab (dashed). Check the pin
  size and the body outline against the drawings of C441236, C441246 and C8406.
- Put the four headers on the board edge, mating face outwards, with room to pull each plug and to reach its screws.
  Keep J7 away from J1 / J5 and mark them on the silkscreen (24 V IN, 24 V OUT, MOTOR, DIR / PWM / GND).
- Put J6 next to J5 and connect J6 pin 3 to J5 pin 2 through the ground pour by a short, wide path, away from the
  ground of the MCU and the sensor: part of the motor current returns that way.
- Give J1 → R23 → Q5 → J5 and the GND return copper for 15 A (see *PCB rules*): pours, 8.3 mm wide on one layer or
  3.2 mm on both layers at once, in 1 oz copper.
  Give the drain tab of Q5 a few cm² of copper. Keep D4 between J5 pin 1 and its ground pin, and C21, D3, R21 close
  to Q5.
- Route Vin+ / Vin- of the INA226 as a Kelvin pair from the inner side of the R23 pads.
- H-bridge: keep each leg's switching loop small. The loop is C26 / C27 → high side → low side → `LS_SRC` → R27 /
  R28 → GND → C26 / C27: put the shunts right against the low-side sources and the capacitors' ground right at the
  GND end of R28, C24 / C25 next to them, VM and GND as pours. The 15 A path is `MOTOR_SW` → JP6 → VM pour → FETs →
  J7, and back from the low-side sources through R27 / R28 to J1 through the ground.
- Route SP / SN as a Kelvin pair from the inner pads (pad 2 of R27, pad 3 of R28) to U7, side by side, away from
  OUT1 / OUT2 and the gate traces. The power current must not flow in them: pads 1 / 4 carry it. Keep R29 / R30 at
  the VREF pin. The FETs lose their heat through the drain pad: give the
  high sides' VM pad and the OUT1 / OUT2 pads of the low sides a few cm² of copper, with vias to the other side.
- Put U7 close to the FETs: short gate traces, each GHx next to its SHx, each GLx over ground. C28 at pin 1, C29
  between pin 2 and VM, C30 across pins 3 / 4, C31 / C32 at pins 7 / 8, the exposed pad soldered to GND with vias.
- U7 uses KiCad's `Texas_RGE0024H` footprint (2.7 mm exposed pad). The DRV8701 datasheet only shows the generic RGE
  package: compare the pad with TI's land pattern or the EasyEDA footprint of C90964 before ordering.
- Leave JP2-JP5 reachable with a knife and JP6 with a soldering iron.
- J4 is an EVERCOM 5301-6P6C (C3097716) on the Amphenol 54601 footprint. Its LCSC 3D model fits: the 0.45 mm pins
  fall in the 0.76 mm holes and the two pegs in the 3.25 mm holes. EasyEDA's own land pattern drills 0.9 mm, so
  check the drilling against the datasheet before ordering.
- 3D models: J3 and J4 use the LCSC models (`kicad_library/pcb/JLCImport.3dshapes`, VRML only: the LCSC STEP files
  have another origin), U2 KiCad's `TI_SO-PowerPAD-8.step`. The plugs of J1 / J5 / J6 / J7
  (`kicad_library/pcb/Desktopia.3dshapes/Plug_*.wrl`) follow Phoenix Contact's figures for the plugs the Kefa and
  KANGNEX ones copy (MSTB 2,5 / GMSTB 2,5: 15 mm high, 18.2 to 18.3 mm long; MC 1,5: 11.1 × 16.1 mm), mated in the
  cavity measured on KiCad's header models. They are a fit check, not a mechanical drawing.

## Known issues and history

1. **TPS5430 ENA (fixed in the schematic).** ENA (pin 5) used to be tied to the 24 V input. Its absolute maximum
   is 7 V, and that burnt the regulator on the first board. ENA is now left open: its internal 1.5 MΩ pull-up keeps
   the regulator on. On a board that met this fault, also check the 5 V parts, since the dead regulator may have
   passed the 24 V through.
2. **Barrel jack (fixed).** The barrel jack was rated 1.5 A / 12 V and carried the motor current. It is replaced by
   the J1 terminal (now a pluggable one, 15 A).
3. **`Mpwm` and `Mdir` pull-downs (fixed in the schematic).** These lines floated during a reset, the bootloader or an
   ISP flash, and the desk could start moving. R26 / R24 (10 kΩ) now hold them low.
4. **Current limit to tune.** The INA226 alert threshold (`MOTOR_CURRENT_LIMIT_MA` in the firmware, 8 A by default)
   is a guess. Measure the real supply current with the `desktopia_debug` build, which logs it during a loaded climb,
   then set the limit at about 1.5 × the peak.
5. The 5 V loop compensation (C4 / R9 / C12 / C11) is calculated, not measured. On the first board, check the 5 V
   ripple and a load step, for example 0.1 → 0.6 A.
6. **VL53L1CB I/O mode.** The ST driver (STM32duino VL53L1X 2.0.1) writes 0 to registers 0x2E / 0x2F, which selects the
   1V8 I/O mode. This board pulls SCL / SDA / GPIO1 up to AVDD (2.8 V), and for that the datasheet (DS12385, table 13)
   asks for the 2V8 mode: set bit 0 of 0x2E (I2C) and 0x2F (GPIO1) after `VL53L1X_SensorInit()`. The original design
   had the same mismatch.
7. **On-board H-bridge (schematic only, not built, not tested).** The firmware still targets the MD10C: it does not
   drive `nSLEEP` (PB5), so the bridge stays asleep, does not read `nFAULT` (PB7), and refuses a PWM above 20 kHz
   (`static_assert` in `motor.cpp`). To do: `nSLEEP` high after `MOTOR_SW` and 1 ms before the first PWM, low before
   Q5 opens; `INPUT_PULLUP` on PB7 and a fault on its falling edge; `MOTOR_PWM_FREQ_HZ` = 25000 (Timer1 TOP = 639).
   With the bridge bypassed (JP2-JP6 cut) the firmware works as before. On the first board, also measure
   V<sub>OFF</sub> on TP14 to know the real current limit (see *H-bridge*).
